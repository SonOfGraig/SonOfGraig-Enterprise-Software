02 · The transformation thesis

From hours to access.

SonOfGraig started life as a digital services agency. Each service was sold as bespoke professional work: hours traded for money, projects scoped and delivered, relationships managed client by client. That model has a fundamental ceiling. The transformation underway changes the unit of value — from hours to access. Each of the eighteen service lines becomes the product logic embedded inside a SaaS platform.

The current state
Services agency — today.

Eighteen service lines. Bespoke project work for South African enterprise clients. Revenue is invoiced project-by-project. Delivery is human, scoped, and individual. Margins are typical of agency work, scale is constrained by headcount, and quality depends on individual talent availability.

  • Project-based invoicing
  • Human-delivered bespoke work
  • Headcount-constrained scaling
  • Typical agency-margin profile
The transformation
Software company — tomorrow.

Same eighteen service lines, repackaged as five SaaS product clusters. Recurring monthly subscriptions plus usage-based metering. Software accessed via dashboard and API. Infrastructure-constrained, not headcount-constrained. The 18 service lines are not abandoned — they become the intellectual property, domain expertise, and product logic embedded inside the platform.

  • Subscription + usage-based metering
  • Dashboard- and API-accessed software
  • Infrastructure-constrained scaling
  • SaaS gross-margin profile at scale

What stays the same: the domain expertise from the eighteen service lines, the existing enterprise client relationships, and the brand reputation for technical depth and enterprise delivery. What changes: how revenue is generated, how value is delivered, and how scaling works.

05 · What we believe

Six convictions that shape every decision.

These are not values painted on a wall. They are the operating principles that decide what we ship, who we hire, how we price, and what we say no to. Each one is a position with a cost — and we accept the cost because the alternative is worse.

01
ZAR pricing eliminates FX risk
SonOfGraig's cost structure is denominated in ZAR. Pricing in USD would create FX risk that we would have to hedge, and that cost would be passed to customers. By pricing in ZAR, your software budget does not move when the rand moves. This is a principle, not a feature.
02
POPIA must be architecture, not a checkbox
Compliance designed in is materially cheaper to operate than compliance bolted on. The eight conditions for lawful processing are enforced in the platform infrastructure — PII scrubbing in middleware, residency at the IAM layer, audit logs that cannot be deleted. Every cluster ships with the same foundation.
03
African enterprise AI deserves local infrastructure
Production AI infrastructure for South African enterprise customers should be built and operated inside South Africa, by people who understand both the technology and the regulatory regime. Adapting an international platform after the fact is not the same as building one for the conditions you actually face.
04
Build in public — including the failures
The roadmap is published with realistic dates, not aspirational ones. Delays are explained. The phase status is visible. We document what we learn as we transition from a services agency to a software company — both what works and what does not.
05
Customers own their source code
Every service engagement ships the implementation as Terraform and application source code committed to the customer's Git, not held by us. There are no proprietary lock-ins. If a customer chooses not to convert to a platform subscription, the system runs on standard open-source components their team can maintain. The relationship is voluntary.
06
Vendor-independent audits stay independent
When SonOfGraig audits an AI system, the engagement is structured so that finding "your existing system is fine" is just as valid an outcome as finding "your existing system needs to be replaced". Most governance audit engagements do not result in a SonOfGraig product purchase. That is a feature, not a bug.

08 · Frequently asked

Questions buyers and journalists tend to ask first.

If you have an additional question that ought to be answered here, send it to hello@sonofgraig.com — we will add it.

Why is the company called SonOfGraig?
The company is named after the founder. Graig is part of the founder's middle name — Tumiso Graig Ramaboya — and SonOfGraig reflects the founder's relationship to his lineage and the responsibility of building under his own name. The name is a deliberate choice: every piece of work the company does is signed by a person, not hidden behind a generic brand. We chose this because the South African enterprise market values knowing exactly who is accountable.
Are you a services company or a software company?
Both, deliberately. Services today, software tomorrow is the operating frame. SonOfGraig delivers fixed-scope service engagements (RAG, AI Agent, Cloud, Audit, Data) right now — that is how the platform build is funded and how customers get value before the platform fully ships. The platform itself is launching in phases over 2026 and 2027. Service customers can convert to platform subscriptions on close. The company is on a deliberate trajectory from one model to the other — not stuck between them.
Who are your customers?
South African enterprise organisations across legal, financial services, professional services, and the public sector. The Phase 1 beachhead targets 10 paying enterprise customers across legal, financial services, and professional services. Customer names are confidential under NDA — we do not publish a logo wall on principle. References are available to qualified buyers under NDA.
Are you VC-funded?
Not currently. SonOfGraig is privately held and the platform build is funded by services revenue. The unit economics of the services line are designed to fund the SaaS build — which is unusual for a software company at this stage and is a deliberate choice. We will update this position openly if and when it changes.
Where is the team based?
Pretoria, South Africa. The registered office is in KwaMhlanga, Mpumalanga, and operations run on Africa/Johannesburg time. Production infrastructure runs in AWS af-south-1 (Cape Town). All customer data is processed and stored in South Africa. Hybrid working is supported for the engineering team; client-facing roles default to Pretoria.
Will you expand outside South Africa?
Yes — in Phase 3 of the roadmap, after the South African platform is mature. The first regional expansion targets are Nigeria and Kenya, with in-country infrastructure presence (additional AWS regions or Cloudflare data localisation) following the same compliance-first principles we apply in South Africa. We will not "expand" by simply opening a sales office in a market we cannot serve compliantly.
What are you most worried about?
Two things. One, the temptation to raise capital and accelerate the roadmap before the unit economics are proven — the realistic timeline is the right timeline. Two, the temptation to compete with AWS, Microsoft, and Datadog on their own turf instead of winning on the local advantage (ZAR pricing, POPIA-native architecture, in-region support). Discipline on both fronts is the difference between a serious software company and another services firm with a SaaS pitch.
How do I work with you?
Three doorways. Service engagements — fixed-scope, fixed-price projects from R45,000 covering RAG, AI Agent, Cloud Architecture, Data Analytics, and Governance Audit. See sonofgraig.com/services. Platform early access — founding-customer terms for the AI Dev Platform, available to organisations that want to shape the product. See /platform/early-access.
